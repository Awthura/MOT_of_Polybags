The shaded columns are the anticipated measurements: what you expect to find, recorded before the solve so the solve can be checked against something. An expected height is the cheapest guard there is against the failure that otherwise goes unnoticed — a board printed at "fit to page", which makes every distance wrong by the scale factor while leaving the reprojection error looking perfectly healthy.

Camera names must match the names the recorder writes its streams under (basler_1, rgbd_2_color, …). A calibration saved under a name nothing else uses is invisible downstream, and nothing will report that — it simply never matches.

An image carries no units. Two things make it a map: where world (0, 0) sits, and what a pixel is worth in millimetres. A .glb supplies both from the model itself (glTF fixes metres and the model origin) and skips this entirely.
